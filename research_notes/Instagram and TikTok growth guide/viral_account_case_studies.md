# Viral meme, mascot and character-IP accounts on TikTok and Instagram: case studies and repeatable patterns (2023 to 2026-10-13)

Research method note (applies to everything below): outbound page fetching was blocked by the network policy in this session (every WebFetch and curl request to Forbes, CoinDesk, Medium, Substack, The Drum, Daily Dot and Adweek failed). As a result, every finding comes from web-search result summaries and snippets, not from reading the full articles. Each claim is tied to the URL the search surfaced. The report writer should treat numbers as "reported by X on date Y", not as audited figures. Many follower and view numbers are self-reported by brands or agencies, and those are flagged. Dates on X/Twitter posts were derived from the post ID (snowflake timestamp).

Survivorship bias warning (applies to the whole file): every case here is a winner that press chose to cover. The research found almost no documentation of mascots, meme pages or memecoins that used the same tactics (unhinged mascots, GIF uploads, lore, daily posting) and got nowhere. The patterns below show what the winners had in common. They do not prove that those things cause virality.

## 1. Crypto-native IP that broke into mainstream short-form (Pudgy Penguins and others)

### Takeaway
Pudgy Penguins is the only crypto-native IP with well-documented, sustained mainstream short-form reach. It got there with a "distribution first, crypto second" playbook: thousands of free GIPHY/Tenor reaction stickers, then character-led Instagram Reels and TikToks that reportedly avoid NFT/crypto talk, then physical toys, a YouTube show and a mobile game. By 2026 it reportedly has about 2M+ Instagram followers, about 800K TikTok followers and tens of billions of GIF views. Its creative team is reportedly 30 to 40 people. Other NFT brands (Doodles, Azuki, BAYC, Milady) have far thinner documented short-form results.

### Cited Findings
**Pudgy Penguins: GIF/sticker distribution (the core engine)**
- Oct 2022: a Pudgy community lead uploaded an initial batch of **50 GIF stickers to GIPHY** and the team added more based on community feedback. By Nov 2022 a team member claimed the GIFs had passed **half a billion views**. The newsletter author cautioned that views ≠ uses, but called it "an effective marketing tool considering evergreen impact and relative low-lift effort." — [TPan, "Short #142: Pudgy Penguin's GIF Strategy"](https://tpan.substack.com/p/short-142-pudgy-penguins-gif-strategy)
- The same newsletter tested generic searches like "Penguin" and "Funny Penguin" in the GIF pickers inside Twitter and Instagram and found Pudgy GIFs ranking prominently, sometimes dominating results. This is the mechanism: GIF search inside other apps puts the character in front of people who have never heard of the brand. — [TPan Short #142](https://tpan.substack.com/p/short-142-pudgy-penguins-gif-strategy)
- A later TPan post reported **over 38 billion GIF views in around two years**. It also cited WonderPals as a comparison: **147 GIFs vs. Pudgy's 1,000+**, with WonderPals at 315M views. This suggests volume of uploads matters. — [TPan #352, "Pudgy Penguins x PENGU marketing"](https://tpan.substack.com/p/352-pudgy-penguins-x-pengu-marketing)
- 2023-11-27 (X post): "The Pudgy Penguins GIFs and stickers on GIPHY have reached **10B views** … our GIFs allow us to cement the Pudgy Penguins IP in internet culture, becoming a brand people interact with every day." — [@pudgypenguins on X](https://x.com/pudgypenguins/status/1729275495273938959) (self-reported)
- PR Daily described the approach as GIFs and "Trojan horsing": building awareness through GIPHY was the first step toward becoming a household toy brand. The article tied the GIF-driven growth plus Walmart distribution to about **$10M in sales**. — [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/?p=342659)

### Inferences
- Pudgy's sequence was: (1) free, searchable GIF/sticker inventory, (2) character Reels/TikToks that work with no crypto context, (3) physical and media products (toys, YouTube, game), (4) token. It treated the crypto layer as the last step, not the hook. For $EMO, the matching move is to make the emo character(s) entertaining to people who will never buy the coin.
- The GIF channel is the cheapest replicable part. A small team can upload hundreds of emotion-tagged reaction stickers ("sad", "crying", "ugh", "mood", "rawr", "eyeliner") to GIPHY. A small brand cannot match the animator headcount, so evergreen GIF search distribution offers the most reach per hour of work.
- Pudgy's reported focus on shares (and Instagram's "sends per reach" signal, see Section 4) suggests content should be designed to be *sent to a friend* ("this is so you"), not just liked.
- Pudgy's numbers come with large inconsistencies (GIF views from 28.9B to 231B depending on source and date). Cite them as "reported," and never quote the PENGU site's numbers as independent fact.

### Gaps
- No primary interview found that states Pudgy's posting cadence (posts/day) on TikTok or Instagram, or which formats perform best. The 30 to 40 person team figure is a third-party paraphrase.
- A secondary claim that a "Kind Pengu" TikTok account had 1.2M followers and 0.5B views across 13 to 14 videos appeared in a search summary with no traceable source. It is excluded as unverified.
- No documented short-form strategy or metrics found for Milady/Remilia. Nothing reliable on Lil Pudgys viewership.
- Social Blade/Similar trackers could not be accessed (fetch blocked), so there are no independent growth curves.

## 2. Memecoins with real TikTok/IG presence: born from short-form virality vs. riding an existing meme

### Takeaway
In every documented 2024 to 2025 case, the viral thing came first and the coin came after. Moo Deng (zoo TikTok), Peanut the squirrel (owner's Instagram/TikTok), Chill Guy (artist's drawing turned TikTok sound trend), dogwifhat (2018 Instagram dog photo), POPCAT (2020 cat meme), GOAT/Fartcoin (an AI bot's X posts), $TRUMP (celebrity). The coins rode attention they did not create. Several were openly opposed by the original creators. None of the coins themselves are documented as having built a large official TikTok or Instagram account. Pudgy/PENGU is the inverse case: IP built first, token later.

### Cited Findings

By Sept 9, fan art was "flooding X daily." — [meme.com, Moo Deng](https://meme.com/memes/moo-deng) (wiki-style, some inconsistencies)

- Mid-Sept 2024: the zoo-linked Moo Deng TikTok account had **2.6M followers** (Reuters-sourced). A Time-cited report said **2M+ TikTok and 200K Instagram**. Weekday visitors rose to 3,000 to 4,000 from a normal ~800. — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/baby-hippo-goes-viral-on-tiktok-doubles-thai-zoos-visitor-count-205918220.html); [Says.com](https://says.com/my/news/viral/thai-zookeepers-surprised-by-viral-fame-of-bouncy-baby-hippo-star)

- Cautionary example: $HAWK ("Hawk Tuah" viral star) launched **Dec 5, 2024**, hit **$490M** and dropped about 95% within minutes. Investors sued the promoters. — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/group-behind-viral-hawk-tuah-stars-meme-coin-hit-lawsuit-over-crash)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: in this period, "memecoin virality" mostly meant attaching a ticker to attention that already existed on TikTok, Instagram or X. The coins' own social accounts are not documented as growing on short-form. The real-world source accounts did (zookeeper, squirrel owner, artist). This means the copyable lesson for $EMO comes from the *source accounts* (a lovable recurring character, raw frequent clips, a strong signature behaviour like Moo Deng's "bouncy" chaos), not from the coin accounts.
- Using someone else's viral character created legal and reputational blowback (Banks, Longo). A brand that *owns* its character (as $EMO does) avoids this and can license it freely, which is Pudgy's model.
- Coins tied to a single viral moment show sharp boom-and-bust curves (TRUMP, HAWK, MOODENG down from peaks). Short-form virality can drive price spikes, but nothing here shows it sustains them.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on the follower counts or posting behaviour of *official* coin accounts (MOODENG, PNUT, CHILLGUY, WIF, BONK, POPCAT, Fartcoin) on TikTok or Instagram. They appear to be primarily X/Telegram-based, but this was not verified account by account.
- Platform rules on crypto promotion are out of scope here and covered by another researcher. They likely explain part of why coin accounts avoid TikTok/IG.
- Exact MOODENG all-time-high market cap is unverified ($71M to $600M+ depending on source and date).

## 3. Non-crypto mascot and character accounts that went viral (Duolingo, Scrub Daddy, Ryanair, Chipotle, Nutter Butter, Grimace, virtual creators, Labubu, Italian brainrot)

### Takeaway
Character-led brand content worked when four things were true. The character had a consistent, exaggerated personality (unhinged, self-aware or surreal). The team was small and fast, with permission to be weird. The comment section was treated as the creative brief, with fans pulled into the story. And there were ongoing storylines or "lore", not one-off ads. Many of the biggest moments (Grimace Shake, Labubu, Chill Guy, Italian brainrot) were started by *users* and amplified by the brand or IP owner, not planted.

### Cited Findings
**Duolingo (Duo the owl)**
- Social lead Zaria Parvez's "unhinged" approach took Duolingo's TikTok from about **50K to 16M followers in a little over four years**. Her mantra: "the comment section is our social brief, and the community really drives what we do." The team was **two full-time employees plus a few contractors**, with supportive legal and executive teams. — [The Drum, Feb 25, 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot)
- Duolingo's CMO said posting daily on social was a big "retention driver" for the business. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/)
- Sept 30, 2026 snapshot: TikTok about **18.2M followers** (+~295K in prior 30 days, 13.7% engagement), YouTube about 6.9M, Instagram about 5.3M. — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/duolingo/) (third-party tracker). Jan 2026: 16.3M TikTok. — [Brand24](https://brand24.com/blog/duolingo-social-media-strategy/)
- "Death of Duo" storyline: on **Feb 11, 2025** Duolingo announced Duo had died (later revealed as "death by Cybertruck"). The X post drew **63.2M views, 68K reposts and 650K likes in one day** — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/events/duolingo-owl-dies). Campaign cited 142M X users reached — [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/duolingos-mascot-death-stunt-genius-confusing/1908041). On **Feb 17** it pointed users to bringback.duolingo.com with a 50B XP goal, and Duo returned a week later. — [NPR, Feb 26, 2025](https://npr.org/2025/02/26/nx-s1-5309785/duolingo-owl-mascot-lives). The storyline began from "a simple app icon change." — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot)

**Nutter Butter (surreal lore)**
- A **Sept 11, 2024** video (Mr. Nutter Butter chasing "Aiden", a real fan who commented on every post) reached about **12.5M views**. The TikTok account grew from **400K to 1.1M followers** afterward. The accounts were run by Zach Poczekaj (Dentsu Creative) with Mondelez brand staff. — [CNN via ABC17, Oct 13, 2024](https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/10/13/nutter-butter-are-you-okay/)
- A Temple graduate who commented daily became part of the social team (Oct 1, 2024). — [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/nation-world/nutter-butter-social-media-aidan-maloney-temple-20241001.html)
- The style was described as "90's internet graphics meets Microsoft paint meets Salvador Dali," with "deep-fried" lo-fi graphics and manipulated sound. A dollhouse video drew 7.6M views. The team built plot lines, characters and a "framework of lore." — [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/nutter-butter-tiktok); [AdNews, "Lore Marketing"](https://adnews.com.au/news/obsessed-lore-marketing-marketing-lore)
- Dentsu Creative says the "Nutterverse" turned fans' predictions and conspiracies into "our next content prompt," with easter eggs and clues. Agency-reported result: a **16.5% increase in household penetration among younger consumers**. — [YouScan interview with Dentsu Creative](https://youscan.io/blog/dentsu-creative-interview/); [The Drum Awards case study](https://www.thedrum.com/awards-case-study/how-nutter-butter-revived-its-brand-with-a-surreal-nutterverse-campaign)

**Grimace Shake (McDonald's, user-generated, brand reacted)**
- The limited Grimace Birthday Shake launched **June 12, 2023**. ## 4. Meme pages that grew fast on Instagram/TikTok in 2024 to 2026: tactics, frequency and formats

### Takeaway
Documented, current growth data for independent meme pages is scarce. Most of the material is vendor blogs or pre-2023 interviews. The best-supported platform facts are these. Instagram's head said in Jan 2025 that watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach** are the key ranking signals, with sends weighted more for reaching non-followers. Photo slideshows/carousels are a major meme vehicle on both apps (the Chill Guy resurgence in Nov 2024 ran on TikTok photo slideshows). Older evidence supports daily posting, running several pages, and getting reshared by bigger accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Jan 2025: Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the most important Instagram ranking factors: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/) (via search summary). Claims that sends carry "3 to 5x" the weight of likes trace to a third-party estimate and are unverified. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-sends-per-reach-playbook/)
- TikTok Photo Mode (slideshows, up to 35 images) launched Sept 2022. Early carousels were "filled with memes, photos and quotes recycled from other platforms." — [Kowatek blog](https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=10590); [Metricool guide, Nov 2025](https://metricool.com/tiktok-slideshow/). Vendor and anecdotal claims say carousels were pushed to non-followers more often in late 2024 and early 2025 due to dwell time. These are unverified.
- The Chill Guy meme's **Nov 2024 resurgence on TikTok ran via photo slideshows**. This is a concrete case of the slideshow format reigniting a character meme. — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/my-new-character-his-whole-deal-is-hes-a-chill-guy-that-lowkey-doesnt-give-a-fuck)
- Instagram carousel advice (vendor blogs, 2025 to 2026): hook on slide one, one idea per slide, a swipe cue, and a final slide asking for a share or comment. "Relatable" meme carousels are cited as having high share rates. — [InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/create-viral-instagram-carousel-types-that-get-millions-of-views-in-2026); [Reelbase](https://reelbase.io/blog/instagram-carousel-reels-complete-guide) (engagement claims not independently verified)
- Background (pre-2023): the Sociaty meme network founder credited **posting every day** for reaching about 5M combined followers and 10B+ impressions a year. @TheTinderBlog's "big break" came when Elite Daily shared one of its memes, after which it grew about **150K followers/month**. The founder ran multiple pages because "a page can be shut down at any point." — [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/joseph-yomtoubian-how-a-business-consultant-created-some-of-the-most-influential-instagram-pages/)
- Platform risk: in 2019 Instagram disabled **142 meme accounts** with millions of combined followers, then sought a partnerships manager for meme accounts. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=134337)
- A niche precedent: Emo Nite (an LA party brand) credits the virality of its memes with growing it to **148K+ Instagram followers**. Its team later launched a creative agency, Ride or Cry. — [Nylon](https://www.nylon.com/emo-nite-la-five-year); [Highsnobiety](https://highsnobiety.com/p/emo-nite-interview)

### Inferences
- For Instagram, design every meme to be *sent* ("tag the friend who…", "this is so you", relatable emo pain), because sends drive reach to non-followers. This lines up with Pudgy's reported focus on shares.
- Use photo slideshows on TikTok and carousels on Instagram as the low-cost workhorse format for a large backlog of meme images. Video can be saved for character moments.
- Spread risk across pages and platforms (main brand page, character page, a pure-meme page without crypto references). The 2019 purge and later crypto-content rules make single-account dependence fragile.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2024 to 2026 interviews or Social Blade data for specific large meme pages (e.g., niche TikTok slideshow pages), and no platform-published posting-frequency benchmarks. The research brief's request for "large meme accounts, niche meme pages" growth tactics is answered only with older or vendor sources.
- Engagement-rate comparisons between carousels and Reels (e.g., "10% vs 6%") come from vendor blogs citing unverified data and are excluded as facts.

## 6. Virtual pet / Tamagotchi-style content: what went viral and why it was shareable

### Takeaway
Virtual pets had a strong 2024 to 2026 run. Tamagotchi Paradise pre-orders reached 4x those of the prior model (Jul 2025). Bongo Cat, a free "idle pet" on Steam, peaked at about 195K concurrent players (2025). Finch, a self-care pet app, grew on word of mouth. Pou became a TikTok vessel for ironic sadness (5B+ hashtag views, 2024), and Webkinz is drawing millennials and Gen Z back to old accounts (2025). Three things made them shareable: emotional projection onto a cute, slightly pathetic creature; nostalgia; and low-effort, always-on interaction that produces screenshots and clips.

### Cited Findings
- **Tamagotchi Paradise** went on sale **Jul 12, 2025**. Pre-orders opened May 21 at **more than 4x the pre-orders of Tamagotchi Uni**, and cumulative Tamagotchi shipments passed **98.1M units** (as of Mar 2025). — [Bandai Namco press release (JP PDF)](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/files/E38090WEBE383BBHDE585B1E69C89E38091E38397E383ACE38.pdf). ### Inferences
- Emogotchi fits an emotional pattern that already exists: Pou content works because people project sadness onto a cute creature, and an emo pet is that idea with a built-in aesthetic. Shareable artefacts like pet states, outfits and rare variants likely give players something to post. Bongo Cat's cosmetic/item mechanics were not verified in this research.
- Nostalgia (Tamagotchi, Webkinz, Pou) overlaps with emo nostalgia (mid-2000s). Both cohorts respond to a 2000s-styled pixel pet.

## 7. Common patterns a small memecoin brand could copy (synthesis, with evidence links)

### Takeaway
Across crypto IP, brand mascots, character memes and virtual pets, the documented winners share seven traits. (1) One consistent, emotionally legible character. (2) Mass free distribution of the character as GIFs, stickers and slideshows. (3) Content that works without knowing the product, and no crypto pitch in the feed. (4) Running lore and storylines. (5) The comment section treated as the brief, with fans written into the story. (6) Participatory formats that let users make their own versions. (7) High posting volume from a small, fast team. The pattern most specific to crypto: the coin rode attention created elsewhere. A brand that owns its character should build character attention first.

### Cited Findings
- **Consistent character, distributed as reaction assets:** Pudgy started with 50 GIPHY stickers (Oct 2022) and reached 1,000+ GIFs and tens of billions of views. It ranked in generic "penguin" GIF search inside other apps. — [TPan Short #142](https://tpan.substack.com/p/short-142-pudgy-penguins-gif-strategy); [TPan #352](https://tpan.substack.com/p/352-pudgy-penguins-x-pengu-marketing)

### Inferences
Each item below is interpretation applied to the $EMO / Emogotchi context, drawn from the evidence above:
1. **Make "emo" a character first and a coin second.** Keep the main TikTok and Instagram feeds free of tickers, charts and "buy" language, as Pudgy reportedly does. Put token talk on X, Telegram and Discord. This also lowers platform-policy risk, which another researcher covers.
2. **Turn the backlog into reaction inventory.** Upload emotion-tagged emo GIFs and stickers to GIPHY and Tenor in volume (hundreds, not dozens), tagged with high-search feelings ("sad", "crying", "mood", "ugh", "it's not a phase", "rawr"). This is the cheapest evergreen distribution with documented scale. The view counts are vanity metrics, but in-app GIF search placement is real reach.
3. **Use slideshows and carousels for meme images and short clips for character beats.** Write every meme to be *sent* ("send this to your emo friend", "POV: elder emo at the MCR show").
4. **Run lore.** Give emo and the Emogotchi pets ongoing storylines: a pet that "dies" if neglected and needs the community to revive it, which mirrors Duo's 50B XP mechanic and ties directly to the game; rival characters; and "are you OK?" arcs. Let comment theories steer the next episode.
5. **Build participatory hooks.** Examples: "make your own emo" (a Doodle-Me-style avatar maker or pet customiser), "rate my Emogotchi" tier lists, a duet/stitch-able signature sound or format, and a recurring commenter who gets written into a video.
6. **Post at high frequency with a tiny team and fast approvals.** Treat posting as daily, not campaign-based. React in character to emo-calendar moments (MCR/Warped/Emo Nite dates, Sept 12 "Black Parade Day"-style anniversaries if verified, viral sad-song trends) within hours, not days.
7. **Respect the subculture.** Celebrate rather than mock, use inclusive character variants, and avoid using band lyrics, artwork or other artists' characters without permission. The Chill Guy and Peanut controversies show the reputational cost of borrowing IP.
8. **Expect power-law outcomes.** The cases here are survivors. Most accounts using these tactics do not break out, so plan for many small bets (high volume, many formats) rather than one hero campaign.

### Gaps
- There is no controlled evidence (A/B or cohort data) that any one tactic causes virality. All patterns are correlational and drawn from winners.
- No documented examples of a *small* crypto memecoin (sub-$50M market cap) building a large TikTok or Instagram following through its own content. This is a gap in the public record. It may also mean the path is rare or unreported.
- "Black Parade Day" style anniversary dates and specific trending emo sounds were not verified in this research and should be checked before use.
