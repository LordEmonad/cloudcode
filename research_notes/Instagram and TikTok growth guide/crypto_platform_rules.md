## 1. TikTok: what the Community Guidelines, FYF standards, branded-content rules and ads rules say about crypto, tokens, financial products, links and contract addresses

### Takeaway
TikTok's Community Guidelines have no rule that names "crypto" or "memecoin" for organic posts. They ban financial scams and "get-rich-quick" schemes, ban "trading, marketing, or providing access to … high-risk goods and services", and require a disclosure toggle on any post promoting your own product. The new version (released Aug 25, 2026, effective Sept 24, 2026) adds a "High-Risk Financial Services" regulated category. Secondary sources say it moves to the Not Allowed list, and memecoin shilling plausibly falls under it. TikTok has banned paid creator promotion ("branded content") of crypto since July 2021. Its ads policy prohibits ads for virtual currencies, apart from licensed exchanges and custodial wallets that TikTok approves. Neither platform has published a rule on posting contract addresses.

### Cited Findings
**Community Guidelines: versions and dates [OFFICIAL]**
- The current TikTok Community Guidelines version is marked "Released August 25, 2026 / Effective September 24, 2026" (the "2026H2update"), and its links now point to a new URL structure (tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/…). — [OTA snapshot of TikTok CG 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md); [TikTok Regulated Goods page](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/regulated-commercial-activities)
- The previous version was "Released August 14, 2025 / Effective September 13, 2025". — [OTA snapshot 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md); [usefastlane summary](https://www.usefastlane.ai/blog/tiktok-community-guidelines)

**Regulated goods, services and commercial activities [OFFICIAL]**
- Summary rule (2026H2): "We don't allow trade, marketing, or providing access to regulated, prohibited, or high-risk goods and services. Verified business accounts and verified TikTok Shop sellers may be allowed to sell or market some regulated items if they meet strict requirements." The 2025 text said "Registered business accounts", and 2026 changed this to "verified business accounts". — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- TikTok's definition of "trade" covers far more than selling: "**Trade:** The sale, purchase, exchange, giveaway, or redirection of goods or services. This includes posting links, sharing contact or location information, or directing people off-platform." — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- In the 2025H2 version, the Not Allowed list for regulated goods covers alcohol, tobacco, drugs, firearms, gambling, "mystery value products", counterfeits, "fake currency", engagement services and similar items. **It does not mention crypto, tokens or financial products by name.** — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- 2026 change: three new regulated categories were added, including "High-Risk Financial Services ('Products or activities involving the management of money and other financial assets that are predatory, contain extreme risks, or lack transparency')". These "join the Not Allowed list", and "Financial education and information about financial products and services" joins the exceptions open to verified businesses. **[SECONDARY]** The OTA snapshot of the 2026 page did not capture the expanded "More information" lists, so I could not check this wording against TikTok's text. — [NoobClaw, Sept 2026 update](https://noobclaw.com/blog/tiktok-community-guidelines-update-september-2026/); [web search summary of TikTok page](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/regulated-commercial-activities)
- Account bans (2026H2): TikTok may ban an account for "Running accounts that are dedicated to breaking the rules, like … the unauthorized trading of regulated, prohibited (e.g. counterfeit), or high-risk goods". It may also "ban all of their accounts, including associated accounts" for serious or evasive violations. — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**Frauds and scams [OFFICIAL]** (identical in 2025H2 and 2026H2)
- Not allowed: "Promoting or helping with scams that target individuals, including: Financial scams, such as fake investment offers or 'get-rich-quick' schemes; Phishing or identity theft; Job or transaction-related scams", plus MLM, money muling and "Trading fake currency". The scope sentence reads: "This includes helping with financial scams, impersonating a celebrity to sell something…, or fraud of any kind." — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**Commercial disclosure, which applies to your own token [OFFICIAL]**
- "Disclosure is required when you're: Promoting your own business, product, or service; Posting branded content… and receiving any kind of incentive." "All branded content must also follow TikTok's Branded Content Policy, Ads Creative Policy, and Industry Entry Policy." — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- **Penalty changed in 2026.** The 2025H2 text said: "If commercial content isn't disclosed using the content disclosure setting, it will be ineligible for the FYF." The 2026H2 text says: "If we find commercial content that hasn't been properly disclosed, we may reduce its visibility or apply the content disclosure setting. Repeated failure… can lead to your account being temporarily restricted from posting content, or… an account ban." — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md) vs [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- The Monetization section says "All commercial content must be disclosed… This applies to content that promotes your brand…", and that branded content must follow the Branded Content Policy, Ads Creative Policy and Industry Entry Policy. It adds: "Content that isn't eligible for the FYF may also be restricted from monetization." — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**For You feed (FYF) eligibility [OFFICIAL]**
- "When we identify content that falls under the 'FYF Ineligible' standards… it won't be recommended in the FYF." The FYF is "intended for a range of audiences that includes everyone from teenagers to great grandparents." — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Items marked FYF-ineligible that matter to a meme brand (2025H2 text): "Tricking others into increasing engagement, such as: 'Like-for-like' promises; False incentives for gifting or following; Misleading claims meant to boost views or popularity". Also "Reused or unoriginal content posted without creative edits, such as clips that show someone else's watermark or logo" and "Low-quality or minimally edited content, such as short clips made from GIFs only". — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- No FYF-ineligible item names crypto or financial content. In both versions I read, the regulated-goods FYF-ineligible lists cover cannabis, tobacco, alcohol, gambling and weapons only. — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- On LIVE: "we reduce visibility of content directing users to purchase products off-platform in markets where TikTok Shop is available" (2025H2 and 2026H2). LIVE gaming streams that "Direct users off-platform to purchase products online" are FYF-ineligible. — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Since a 2023 change, accounts that repeatedly post FYF-ineligible content can themselves be made temporarily ineligible for the FYF. **[SECONDARY news]** — [AOL/Yahoo report on 2023 change](https://www.aol.com/tiktok-start-banning-problematic-content-014504565.html); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/tiktok-new-recommendation-guidelines-penalties-update_id157461)

**External links [OFFICIAL]**
- "Creators often share links in their bios, profiles, or videos… some lead to harmful content that isn't allowed on TikTok. If a link breaks our rules, we'll remove it. We may also temporarily stop you from posting links—or, for severe violations, ban your account." — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Comments with "Spam: Random text, irrelevant promotions, or links" are sorted lower. — [OTA 2026H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**Branded Content Policy, for paid creator partnerships [SECONDARY, multiple outlets]**
- In July 2021 TikTok added "financial services and products" to the "globally prohibited industries" on its branded-content policy page. The list includes "trading platforms, cryptocurrency, foreign exchange", BNPL, loans, "get rich quick" schemes and pyramid schemes. — [International Adviser, 9 Jul 2021](https://international-adviser.com/tiktok-imposes-global-ban-on-investment-promotions/); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/tiktok-bans-crypto-promotions-amid-fears-young-users/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/no-more-dogecoin-or-memecoin-shilling-on-tiktok-after-it-bans-paid-crypto-promotions/)
- Coverage said the ban "appears to only apply to sponsored content, not general videos discussing cryptocurrencies". — [Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/tiktok-bans-financial-services-ads-pyramid-scheme-crypto-bitcoin-103438272.html)
- **Conflict:** A 2026 blog says TikTok revised its Branded Content Policy (dated 31 Aug 2026). It lists financial services as a *restricted* industry, shown only in the market where it was posted and possibly age-limited, rather than a prohibited one, while predatory offers such as payday loans stay banned. Another 2026 creator blog says financial services "are still allowed but face stricter compliance". Neither could be checked against TikTok's own page. — [NoobClaw, Branded Content Policy 31 Aug 2026](https://noobclaw.com/blog/tiktok-branded-content-policy-2026/); [Cleva blog](https://www.getcleva.com/blog/what-does-the-new-tiktok-update-mean-for-you-as-a-content-creator-in-2026)

**Ads policy [OFFICIAL, via search summary]**
- TikTok's Financial Services ads policy lists "virtual currencies, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum" and "high-risk investment products" among prohibited items. "Ads for cryptocurrency exchange services and custodial wallets may be allowed" if the advertiser is licensed by local or regional authorities, works with a TikTok Sales Representative through an application, and targets 18+ only. — [TikTok Business Help Center: Financial Services](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-financial-services)
- TikTok first tested crypto ads in a beta in March 2022. The beta excluded influencer/branded content. — [Marketing Brew, 10 Mar 2022](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/03/10/tiktok-is-beta-testing-a-program-to-allow-crypto-ads)
- Market availability varies by country. One 2026 comparison says TikTok allows crypto ads "market by market, with a local license and sales-team approval". **[SECONDARY]** — [FindClout 2026](https://findclout.com/blog/crypto-advertising-restrictions-2026)

**Contract or wallet addresses**
- I found no TikTok rule, official or secondary, that mentions contract addresses or wallet addresses. The closest official hooks are the "trade" definition ("redirection… directing people off-platform"), the "high-risk goods and services" rule, and the financial-scams rule. — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)

### Inferences
- A ticker and CA in a TikTok bio is not named as a violation anywhere. In TikTok's own terms, though, a CA together with "buy" language is "redirection… directing people off-platform" to acquire a financial product. Since Sept 24, 2026, if moderators treat a memecoin as a "high-risk financial service", that combination looks like "marketing, or providing access to" a high-risk product. The bigger danger is at account level: an account whose bio, captions and comments are all token-focused fits the ban trigger "accounts that are dedicated to… the unauthorized trading of… high-risk goods."
- **The disclosure trap:** posts promoting your own token need the commercial disclosure toggle. Once toggled, commercial content is tested against the Ads Creative and Industry Entry policies, which prohibit virtual currencies. If the toggle is skipped, visibility can be reduced (2026 text). Either way, overtly promotional token posts are disadvantaged. A main account that posts emo meme or character entertainment and does not promote the token avoids both branches, because entertainment content about your own character is not "commercial content" in the same sense. Exactly where that line falls is a judgment call (see Gaps).
- "Price talk", "100x", "going to the moon" and holder-gain screenshots are the content most likely to read as "get-rich-quick" or "misleading claims meant to boost views", whether or not the token is legitimate.
- Holder-led "like and follow to win $EMO" posts would hit the FYF-ineligible "false incentives for… following". A token giveaway may also count as a regulated-goods "trade", since TikTok's definition includes "giveaway".

### Gaps
- I could not read TikTok's 2026 "More information" definitions, which hold the exact High-Risk Financial Services wording and examples, or check whether crypto or memecoins are listed as examples. Only a secondary blog describes the category.
- I could not access TikTok's current Branded Content Policy page. Whether crypto is still "globally prohibited" or now "restricted by market" after Aug 2026 is unresolved, and sources conflict.
- I found no official or reported TikTok rule on contract addresses specifically.
- I could not verify TikTok's current requirements for clickable bio links (commonly said to be business accounts or 1,000+ followers) or any TikTok domain blocklist covering DEX, launchpad or wallet sites.

## 2. Instagram/Meta: Community Standards, Recommendation Guidelines, branded content, links and ads rules for crypto

### Takeaway
Meta's Community Standards do not ban crypto content. They ban specific deceptive patterns: guaranteed or "risk-free" returns, referencing past returns to imply future ones, quick-return claims, "unrealistic financial reward for… minimal effort", cash or cash-equivalent giveaways for engagement, and deceptive links. Recommendations are held to a stricter bar than removals. Secondary sources say Meta's Recommendation Guidelines exclude "misleading or deceptive business models, such as payday loans or 'risk-free' investments", and Instagram can make a whole account non-recommendable based on its content *or profile components*. Paid promotion is the hard gate. Branded content (paid partnerships) for crypto needs Meta's prior written permission, and so do ads for crypto trading, swapping or staking services.

### Cited Findings
**Community Standards, Fraud, Scams and Deceptive Practices [OFFICIAL, verbatim from OTA snapshot]**
- Rationale: Meta prohibits "investment schemes (including guaranteed returns)… get-rich-quick and giveaway schemes". Enforcement can include "removing content and ads, reducing distribution, restricting advertising accounts, limiting features, requiring verification, or disabling accounts." — [OTA Meta Community Standards](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- "**Prohibited Investment Content**" covers: "Offers of investment opportunities where returns on investment are guaranteed or risk-free"; "…where returns… [are] based on recruitment of others"; "**Offers of investment opportunities claiming or referencing successful past performance or returns to create an expectation of similar future results**"; "Offers of investment opportunities that claim quick returns (such as under 24 hours)"; and "cash flip"/"money flip" offers. — [OTA Meta Community Standards](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- "**Prohibited Get Rich Quick Content**" is defined as "Offers of opportunities of unrealistic financial reward for unclear or minimal effort." — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- "**Prohibited Brand or Celebrity-Bait Content**" covers falsely claiming to represent a business or using a public figure's likeness "together with misleading tactics to bait people… to scam or defraud." — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Under the "require additional context" provisions, Meta "may remove content that… Involves entities participating in or claiming to engage in organized fraud or scam behavior, including the use of multiple accounts on our services in concert". — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Meta's gambling rules define "real money" to include "virtual currencies such as Bitcoin". This is the only place the standards name a cryptocurrency. — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**Community Standards, Spam (links and engagement) [OFFICIAL]**
- Prohibited: "Offering giveaways (i.e., offering others a chance to win) of cash or cash equivalents in exchange for engagement (e.g., 'Anyone that likes my page will be entered to win $500')" and "Offering to provide anything of monetary value in exchange for engagement". — [OTA Meta Community Standards](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Prohibited link behaviours: cloaking; "Misleading Links"; "Deceptive redirect behavior"; "Like/share-gating"; "Deceptive landing page functionality"; and "Landing page or domain impersonation", meaning a landing page that "pretends to be a reputable brand… using… typos, misspellings". — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Explicitly allowed: "Cross promotion that is not triggered by payment to a third party" and "clearly identified Branded Content". — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- High-frequency posting or engagement, "either manually or automatically", is prohibited, and restrictions can apply at lower frequencies "when other indicators of Spam (e.g., posting repetitive content)… are present." — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- The legacy Instagram Community Guidelines said: "Don't offer money or giveaways of money in exchange for likes, followers, comments or other engagement." These guidelines moved into Meta's unified Community Standards on Nov 12, 2024 ("None of the standards will change"). — [OTA legacy Instagram Community Guidelines](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/Instagram/Community%20Guidelines.md)

**Recommendation Guidelines (what is "not eligible for recommendation") [OFFICIAL framework + SECONDARY on the financial item]**
- Instagram's help page says Recommendations Guidelines "are designed to maintain a higher standard than our Community Standards, because recommended content and connections are from accounts you haven't chosen to follow". They aim to avoid recommending "low-quality, objectionable, or particularly sensitive" content even when it is not removable. — [Instagram Help: Recommendations on Instagram](https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232/)
- **Account-level effect:** "if your account is not eligible to be recommended, none of your content will be recommended". Professional accounts can check this in Account Status, which can show "a sample of content or components of your profile that may go against" the guidelines. **[Official text quoted via third-party site]** — [Intellicoach summary of Instagram Help](https://intellicoach.ai/blog/instagram-not-eligible-for-recommendations-non-followers); [TechCrunch, 7 Dec 2022 (Account Status launch)](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/07/instagrams-new-transparency-tools-will-tell-you-if-your-content-is-ineligible-to-be-recommended/)
- Meta's Recommendation Guidelines exclude, under "sensitive or low-quality content about health or finance", content that "promotes misleading or deceptive business models", with payday loans as the example. Instagram has "a similar list". **[SECONDARY, 2020]** — [Social Media Today (2020)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-provides-an-overview-of-content-that-it-looks-to-exclude-from-its/584442/). A 2026 summary repeats this with "payday loans or 'risk-free' investments" as examples. — [Buffer 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/facebook-algorithm/)

**Branded content (paid partnerships) [OFFICIAL, via search summary]**
- Meta's Branded Content Policies require the sponsoring business partner to "get written permission from Facebook before promoting cryptocurrency products and services". The same rule covers dating, real-money gambling and pharmacies. — [Meta Branded Content Policies](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254)

**Ads [OFFICIAL]**
- Meta Advertising Standards: "Ads may not promote cryptocurrency trading platforms, software and related services and products that enable monetisation, reselling, swapping or staking of cryptocurrencies without prior written permission." Separately, financial-product advertisers "may be required to verify their business and/or individual identity and demonstrate that they are authorised by the relevant regulatory authorities". — [OTA snapshot of Meta Advertising Standards](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Advertising%20Content%20Policy.md); [Meta Transparency Center: Cryptocurrency Products and Services](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/cryptocurrency-products-and-services/)
- History: Meta banned crypto ads in January 2018. On Dec 1, 2021 it raised the number of accepted regulatory licences from 3 to 27 and published the list, so licensed advertisers no longer needed a bespoke application. — [The Block, 1 Dec 2021](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2021-12-01-meta-has-made-it-easier-to-run-crypto-related-ads-on-its-platform-126035); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hdtc/facebook-relaxes-cryptocurrency-advertising-requirements)
- **[SECONDARY, conflicting]** Blogs claim a 2026 tiered authorisation and "Verified Crypto Advertiser" badge (March 2026), and say mining hardware no longer needs permission (April 2026). Other blogs say no public badge exists. None of this was confirmed against Meta. — [Blockchain-ads 2026](https://www.blockchain-ads.com/post/meta-cryptocurrency-advertising-policy); [AuditSocials 2026](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-policy-updates-2026-guide)

**Contract addresses in bio**
- No Meta policy mentions contract or wallet addresses. I found no reporting of Instagram removing or blocking bios for containing one. — (searches returned nothing; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- Instagram treats a CA in the bio as text, with no rule against it. But recommendation eligibility explicitly considers "components of your profile". A bio reading "$EMO | CA: 0x81A2…7777 | buy on [DEX]" makes the whole account look like a financial promotion. That raises the odds of account-level "not eligible to be recommended", mainly through user reports and classifiers. This is inference; Meta publishes no such classifier rule.
- The sentence in Meta's text most likely to bite a memecoin account is "claiming or referencing successful past performance or returns to create an expectation of similar future results". Posts like "$EMO did 10x last month, don't miss the next leg" or PnL screenshots fit it almost verbatim.
- A token airdrop for likes, follows or comments is very likely a "cash equivalent… in exchange for engagement" under the Spam policy, because tokens have market value. This is the highest-risk growth tactic on Instagram.
- Linking to your own website is fine. Links that redirect through several domains, use cloaking or shorteners, or go straight to a wallet-connect page that mimics a known brand would fall under the Spam link rules.

### Gaps
- I could not read Meta's current Recommendation Guidelines page directly. The "risk-free investments / payday loans" exclusion rests on secondary summaries from 2020 and 2026, and I could not confirm whether "contests or giveaways" are on the not-recommended list.
- I found no evidence that Instagram blocks specific crypto domains (DEX Screener, launchpads, wallet sites) in bios or stories.
- The "change log" dates for Meta's Fraud and Spam sections were not captured, so I cannot date when the investment-content bullets were last revised.

## 3. Observed enforcement 2021–2026: what got memecoin/crypto accounts suppressed or banned

### Takeaway
I found **no credible reporting** of a TikTok or Instagram sweep against memecoin accounts in 2024–2026, and no documented case of a ban caused solely by a CA in a bio. Documented enforcement clusters around four things: (a) TikTok's 2021 branded-content ban, which removed paid crypto promos and temporarily banned some creators; (b) Meta's large removals of scam and "pig-butchering" accounts and its scam-ad problem; (c) hijacked celebrity accounts pushing fake memecoins, which ended in account disablement; and (d) regulator-driven takedowns of finfluencer posts, especially in the UK. Claims of "shadowbans" of crypto creators are anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- **[ENFORCEMENT, 2021]** After TikTok's July 2021 branded-content change, crypto creators running paid promotions "saw videos flagged and removed, and some accounts were temporarily banned", including some who said they posted educational content. — [CNBC, 25 Aug 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/08/25/tiktok-policy-change-hurt-cryptocurrency-creators.html)
- **[ANECDOTE]** A crypto influencer is quoted complaining that "TikTok is shadow banning and censoring crypto content creators". This is a claim, not documented evidence. — [Bitrates](https://www.bitrates.com/news/p/tiktok-bans-financial-advice-what-does-this-policy-mean-for-crypto)
- **[OFFICIAL position, via secondary]** TikTok says it does not "shadowban". Reported reach drops typically last "a few days to two weeks". — [Shopify NZ, TikTok shadow ban 2026](https://www.shopify.com/nz/blog/tiktok-shadow-ban)
- **[ENFORCEMENT, Aug 2025]** Hackers took over the Instagram accounts of Michael Jackson (estate), Adele, Tyla and Future to promote a fake Solana memecoin. The posts were removed and Future's account was "ultimately disabled". — [Mitrade/news, 23 Aug 2025](https://www.mitrade.com/insights/news/live-news/article-3-1063899-20250823)
- **[ENFORCEMENT]** Meta removed about 2 million accounts tied to "pig-butchering" (romance plus crypto-investment) scams (reported Nov 2024). It also removed more than 23,000 Facebook Pages and accounts in investment-scam clusters targeting Brazil and India. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/meta-removes-2-million-accounts-related-pig-butchering-scams-rcna181025); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/corporate/meta-removes-over-23000-facebook-pages-accounts-linked-to-investment-scams)
- **[ENFORCEMENT context, Nov 2025]** Reuters reported internal Meta documents estimating about 10% of 2024 revenue (about $16B) came from scam and banned-goods ads, including "fake investment schemes". Meta disputed the figure as "rough and overly-inclusive" and says it removed over 134M scam ads in 2025, with user scam-ad reports down 58% over 18 months. US senators asked the FTC and SEC to investigate. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-s-16-billion-scam-ad-problem); [MediaNama, Nov 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-meta-billions-dollars-scam-ads-internal-documents/); [Blumenthal/Hawley release](https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/newsroom/press/release/blumenthal-and-hawley-call-for-federal-investigation-of-metas-profiting-from-scams_fraud)

## 5. Legal and regulatory disclosure: promoting a token you created or hold (US FTC/SEC, UK FCA, EU MiCA) and what disclaimers actually do

### Takeaway
In the **US**, the SEC staff said on Feb 27, 2025 that typical meme coins are not securities, but fraud and manipulation remain actionable. The FTC's Endorsement Guides require any paid or connected promoter, including holders, to clearly disclose the connection. As the issuer, your own account is the advertiser and is directly liable for deceptive claims. In the **UK**, memes are explicitly capable of being financial promotions. Since Oct 8, 2023, crypto promotions to UK consumers must go through the FCA regime, so unapproved promotion by an unauthorised person can be a criminal offence. In the **EU**, MiCA requires marketing communications to be identifiable, fair, clear, not misleading and consistent with a white paper, and ESMA treats influencer posts as possible marketing or solicitation. A "not financial advice" line cures none of this. What matters is not making return claims, disclosing connections, and not targeting UK/EU consumers with calls to buy.

### Cited Findings
**US**
- On Feb 27, 2025 the SEC Division of Corporation Finance stated that meme coins (as typically offered) are not securities, so offers need no 1933 Act registration. The analysis depends on the facts, and the statement does not settle fraud or manipulation liability, which other federal or state agencies can still pursue. — [Finextra](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/45581/sec-clarifies-regulatory-status-of-meme-coins); [Latham & Watkins fintech blog, Mar 2025](https://www.fintechanddigitalassets.com/2025/03/sec-staff-clarifies-that-meme-coins-are-not-securities/); [NYU compliance blog](https://wp.nyu.edu/compliance_enforcement/tag/jenny-cieplak)
- FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023): a "material connection" (payment, free product, affiliate commission, and per practitioners "even stock ownership") must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously". Practitioners note that platform "paid partnership" tags alone may not suffice. **[SECONDARY practitioner summaries]** — [BatesonLaw](https://batesonlaw.com/ftc-influencer-disclosure-endorsement-guidelines/); [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/ftc-guidelines-for-influencers/)
- In Oct 2022 Kim Kardashian paid the SEC $1.26M for touting a crypto token (EMAX) without disclosing a $250K payment. That was SEC anti-touting under securities law, not the FTC. — [cited in JoinBrands summary](https://joinbrands.com/blog/ftc-guidelines-for-influencers/)
- **$HAWK case.** In Dec 2024 Burwick Law sued the HAWK token's promoters and launch entities (not Haliey Welch herself) in E.D.N.Y. The suit alleged the token was an unregistered security, with $151K+ in plaintiff losses. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/297943/hawk-tuah-hailey-welch-meme-coin-lawsuit); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/hawk-memecoin-draws-lawsuit); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/332019/hawk-tuah-girl-haliey-welch-responds-lawsuit-alleging-hawk-memecoin-securities-law)

**UK**
- The UK cryptoasset financial-promotions regime took effect Oct 8, 2023. — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/interface/2025/regulating-cryptos/uk-rules-on-promoting-cryptoassets); [Latham, Feb 2026](https://www.fintechanddigitalassets.com/2026/02/fca-takes-first-enforcement-action-under-uk-crypto-marketing-regime/)

Unauthorised influencers promoting without approval "may be committing a criminal offence." — [FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/fg24-1-finalised-guidance-financial-promotions-social-media); [Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-kingdom/insights/guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-finalised-guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media/)

**EU (MiCA)**
- MiCA Article 7: crypto-asset marketing communications must be "clearly identifiable" as marketing, "fair, clear and not misleading", and consistent with the white paper. Competent authorities get *notification* but may not impose prior *authorisation*. — [ESMA Interactive Single Rulebook, Art. 7](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-7-marketing-communications); [Manimama](https://manimama.eu/crypto-advertising-under-mica-new-eu-compliance-reality/); [Springer chapter on MiCA white papers and marketing](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-74889-9_12)

ESMA's MiCA-review response asks lawmakers for stricter influencer advertising rules. ### Inferences
- **What matters more than a disclaimer:** (1) never make return, price or "early = rich" claims; (2) make your own account's connection obvious (it's the official brand, which it is) and require any paid creator or KOL to disclose with platform tools plus words ("#ad – paid by emo"); (3) holders who post for you in exchange for tokens or bounties are paid endorsers and must disclose; (4) don't run "buy" calls aimed at UK users (geo-sensitive language, no UK-targeted promos) unless an FCA-authorised firm approves them; (5) for EU reach, any token-promotional post is a MiCA "marketing communication". Keeping the main accounts to culture and entertainment, and putting token facts on a website with risk language, minimises exposure.
- "NFA/DYOR" is theatre as a legal shield. Regulators (ESMA explicitly) look through it. A short risk line ("memecoin, no utility promise, can go to zero") is more meaningful because it balances the message, which is what FG24/1 asks for.

## 6. Giveaways, contests and airdrops: platform rules and crypto interaction

### Takeaway
Both platforms penalise "something of value in exchange for engagement". Meta's Spam policy prohibits giveaways of "cash or cash equivalents in exchange for engagement". TikTok makes "like-for-like" and "false incentives for… following" FYF-ineligible and counts a "giveaway" as a form of regulated "trade". Token airdrops for likes or follows are therefore the riskiest growth tactic on both platforms. Prizes that aren't financial (merch, Emogotchi cosmetics, art features), skill-based meme contests, and entry that doesn't depend on engagement sit on much safer ground.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta [OFFICIAL]:** prohibited: "Offering giveaways… of cash or cash equivalents in exchange for engagement", "Offering to provide anything of monetary value in exchange for engagement", and "Like/share-gating". — [OTA Meta Community Standards](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- **Meta [OFFICIAL]:** "Prohibited Giveaway Content" covers "False offers of a guaranteed reward of real money or consumer goods… without any action required by the user beyond sharing personal or financial information." — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- **Meta [OFFICIAL]:** the Community Standards exempt "brand giveaways" by legitimate entities in the regulated-goods context. — [same](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/Facebook/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- **Instagram Promotion Guidelines [SECONDARY summaries]:** hosts must include "a complete release of Instagram by each entrant" and an acknowledgement that the promotion "is not sponsored, endorsed or administered by" Instagram. Hosts must not tag or encourage tagging people in content they're not in. Enforcement is usually reactive, after reports. Requiring a purchase can turn a sweepstakes into an illegal lottery. — [Woobox](https://woobox.com/articles/instagram-giveaway-rules-and-compliance); [ContestQueen 2024](https://www.contestqueen.com/2024/conflicting-meta-promotional-guidelines/); [RevUp 2026](https://revup.com/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules-2026/)
- **TikTok [OFFICIAL]:** FYF-ineligible: "'Like-for-like' promises; False incentives for gifting or following; Misleading claims meant to boost views or popularity" (2025H2). "Trade" includes "giveaway". — [OTA 2025H2](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- **TikTok Shop [OFFICIAL, Shop-specific]:** giveaways must be free to enter, items valued under $500, with no cash or gift cards, run through TikTok Shop's LIVE Giveaway feature, and "Gamification is not allowed". — [TikTok Shop Giveaway and Promotions Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=10017760)
- **[SECONDARY, unverified]** Third-party guides report a TikTok giveaway-policy update on Feb 25, 2026: official terms must appear on the video itself, and fake urgency, "prize for every entry", excessive tagging and false partnership claims are banned. — [Moras.ai 2026](https://moras.ai/blog/tiktok-giveaway); [Gleam](https://gleam.io/blog/tiktok-giveaway-rules/)

### Inferences
- "Follow + like + tag 3 friends to win 1M $EMO" breaks Meta's spam rule (a token with market value is a cash equivalent) and TikTok's FYF rule (false incentive for following). It also looks exactly like the wallet-drainer "airdrop" scams that users report. Avoid it.
- Safer formats: (a) a meme or art contest judged on creativity, with merch, an Emogotchi in-game cosmetic or a feature on the account as the prize, published official rules, no purchase or holding requirement, and the Instagram release language; (b) any token distribution kept off TikTok and Instagram entirely (website, X or Telegram), with no "follow us on IG" requirement.

- I could not confirm whether Meta's Recommendation Guidelines list "contests or giveaways" as not-recommended.
- I could not confirm TikTok's official non-Shop giveaway rules (the Feb 2026 update is reported only by third parties).

### Takeaway
Both platforms frame crypto enforcement mainly as anti-scam: impersonation, celebrity/brand bait, phishing links, fake giveaways, and coordinated multi-account fraud. ## 8. Synthesis: the safest practical setup for a memecoin brand account (emo / $EMO)

### Takeaway
Run the TikTok and Instagram accounts as an **emo character/entertainment brand** (memes, Emogotchi clips, lore), with no price talk, no buy calls and no engagement-for-tokens. Keep the ticker and CA **off the bio or minimised**, and route to an owned website that holds the CA, risk language and official links. Handle token community and announcements on X, Telegram or Discord, or on a clearly labelled secondary account, and follow disclosure rules strictly. ### Inferences
**A recommended tiered setup (inference from the findings above):**
- **Tier 1, lowest risk (recommended for the main TikTok and Instagram accounts):**
  - Display name "emo" (not "$EMO"). Bio: "emo-core memes and Emogotchi, your sad little pet 🖤".
  - Link: owned website (emo site or link page on your own domain) that holds the CA, chain, "official links" and risk text.
  - Content: memes, character skits, Emogotchi gameplay. No ticker in captions, no price, no charts.
  - Disclosure toggle not needed for pure entertainment. Use it if a post clearly promotes the game or token.
- **Tier 2, moderate risk (what many accounts do):**
  - Main account as Tier 1, but with "$EMO on Monad" in the bio and no CA.
  - Occasional, properly disclosed "your brand" posts about the game or token, with no return claims.
  - Expect lower or variable reach on those posts.
- **Tier 3, highest risk:**
  - Ticker plus full CA in the bio, "buy"/"next 100x" captions, PnL or chart posts, engagement-for-token airdrops, links to DEX or wallet-connect pages, raids or coordinated holder accounts.
  - Each element maps to a specific official rule above (get-rich-quick, past-performance claims, cash-equivalent giveaways, trade/redirection, organized multi-account behaviour). Together they risk FYF/recommendation ineligibility at account level and, on TikTok, a ban as an account "dedicated to" high-risk trading.
- **If two accounts are used:** a culture account ("emo") plus a token/community account ("emo community" or "$EMO holders"). Never cross-post content that got the token account restricted, which is circumvention on TikTok. Link both accounts from the website.
- **Paid creators or KOLs:**
  - TikTok: crypto branded content was prohibited (2021) and may now be restricted by market (unverified 2026 change). Treat paid TikTok creator shills for $EMO as off-limits unless TikTok's current Branded Content Policy confirms otherwise.
  - Instagram: crypto branded content needs Meta's prior written permission.
  - Anywhere: FTC disclosure is required for paid or token-compensated posts.
- **Ads:** effectively unavailable for a memecoin itself on both platforms. TikTok prohibits virtual-currency ads, and Meta requires written permission or licences tied to trading, swapping or staking services. Ads for the browser game (Emogotchi) without token references may be a separate, viable path, but this was not verified.

### Gaps
- No empirical reach comparison (CA in bio vs no CA) exists in the sources found. The tiering above is a risk ranking derived from policy text, not measured outcomes.
- Whether Emogotchi ads would pass review if the game has any token integration was not researched.
