# EMO's upside waits on Monad's spark

The evidence supports a narrower version of your case than the one you are making. Since its **February 25, 2026 all-time low, $EMO has made higher lows and a marginally higher high, and it has beaten MON by a wide margin**. The "higher highs and higher lows for about 10 months" claim fails, though, because that February low sat 77–84% below late-2025 prices. EMO has also **underperformed MON since April**, and the claims that EMO leads volume and that its early buyers are new money cannot be tested with the data available. At about **$0.0008 (roughly $800K market cap) on October 11**, EMO is 56% below its September 6 high and 2.5x above the **$0.000318 double bottom** that holds up its uptrend. A 100x would take EMO to $80–100M, the peak reached by the best flagships on chains other than Solana and Base (HIPPO, COQ, PURR), and each of those then fell 94–99%. A 1000x (about $1B) would value EMO at **roughly three times all circulating MON**, and memes have only reached that size on Solana and Base, during full meme seasons with tier-1 exchange listings. My order-of-magnitude odds, if Monad gets a real meme season, are about 15–30% for a 10x, 2–5% for a 100x and under 0.5% for a 1000x. They are lower without one. Monad has the capital (about $1B of DeFi TVL) but not the spark that starts a meme season. MON trades flat to its sale price, daily active addresses are down about 90% from launch, Monad memes have shrunk to micro-caps, and a **~16.7B MON unlock, about 1.4 times the current float, lands on November 24**. Most on-chain data (daily candles, pool depth, holder concentration) could not be fetched, so the chart verdicts rest on aggregator swing points rather than a daily price series.

## Higher lows date from February, not from launch

EMO is a nad.fun token. Its contract is **0x81A224F8A62f52BdE942dBF23A56df77A10b7777**, with 1B fixed supply, fully circulating. It launched in Monad mainnet's first four days and has since graduated to DEX pools, mainly Uniswap V4 and Capricorn on Monad ([LordEmonad/Emonad README](https://github.com/LordEmonad/Emonad/blob/master/README.md); [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/emonad)). In launch week it rose **290% in a day to about $0.00057**, briefly becoming the #2 Monad meme. The move was reportedly linked to a reply from Monad co-founder Keone Hon ([Phemex News](https://phemex.com/news/article/meme-token-emo-on-monad-chain-soars-290-in-24-hours-40336)). It then bled to an **all-time low of $0.0000929 on February 25, 2026**, a market cap of about $93K ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/price/emonad)). That is a clear lower low, 84% below the November spike, inside the ten-month window you describe. "Higher highs and higher lows for ten months" is therefore not true as stated.

What is true is the structure since that bottom. The **July 25 low of $0.000318 is 3.4x the February low** ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/emonad)), and the **September 6 high of $0.001802 sits 8% above April's $0.001663** ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/price/emonad)). On multi-month swings, that is higher lows and a higher high over about 7.5 months, with EMO still 8.6x off its low. The higher high is fragile. It compares CryptoRank's April print with CoinGecko's September print, and MEXC shows an undated all-time high of $0.00187653 ([MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/price/emonad)). If that was the April print, September was a **4% lower high**. The September "higher low" is also really a double bottom: CoinGecko's weekly AUD data puts the September 2 low at about US$0.00031–0.00032, essentially the July low ([CoinGecko EMO/AUD](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/emonad/aud)).

The chart is better described as **two pump-and-retrace cycles than as a staircase**. EMO ran from February to April (+1,689%), lost 81% into July, ran from July to September (+467%), and has lost 56% since. The September leg was especially compressed. EMO went from about $0.00032 on September 2 to $0.0018 on September 6, **roughly 5.6x in four days**, on about **$143K of 24-hour volume** on the peak day ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/emonad)). Since April, every recorded price has stayed between about $315K and $1.8M of market cap. The uptrend stays intact while **$0.000318 holds on a daily close**. A close below it would be the first clear break since February. Failing to reclaim about $0.0018 would confirm a range rather than a trend.

Thin liquidity is why the swings are this violent. The only dated pool reading is **about $54K of liquidity at a $443K market cap**, from launch week ([DexScreener](https://dexscreener.com/monad/0x714a2694c8d4f0b1bfba0e5b76240e439df2182d)). Current depth could not be retrieved. With $54K in a constant-product pool, **a $10K buy moves the end price about +88% and a $50K buy about +713%**. The same math works in reverse: a $10K sale cuts price by about 47% and returns only about $7.3K. Concentrated-liquidity pools are deeper near the current price but run out faster once an order moves past it. In practice, a single five-figure wallet can print or erase any swing point in the table above. It also means a large holder can only exit meaningful size during volume bursts.

## EMO beats MON on long windows and follows the chain's bursts rather than leading them

The outperformance claim holds on long windows. From the November 28 spike to October 11, **EMO is up about 40% while MON is down about 13%**, which puts the EMO/MON ratio up 62%. From the February lows, **EMO is up 761% against MON's 59%** ([MetaMask MON](https://metamask.io/price/monad); [MEXC News](https://www.mexc.com/news/709431)). Two qualifiers matter. First, the edge reverses on recent windows. From its April peak EMO is down 52% while MON is down 10–21%, and over the past month EMO fell while MON rose about 12%. Second, beating MON is a low bar. MON is roughly flat against its $0.025 Coinbase sale price, about 47% below its $0.0488 all-time high, and down about 35% year on year ([Coinbase MON](https://www.coinbase.com/price/monad); [Bybit](https://www.bybit.com/en/price/monad/)). The EMO/MON ratio has the same shape as EMO's USD chart: a lower low in February, then higher swings. That points to very high beta to Monad sentiment, with token-specific spikes on top. The latest week gives no clear signal. MetaMask implies MON fell about 24% in the week to October 11, while EMO slipped about 11% from October 2. EMO neither caught MON's earlier strength nor matched its drop, but the timestamps do not line up exactly.

"Catches bids whenever volume returns" fits the coarse timeline. EMO's three big moves all coincided with Monad-wide activity bursts. The first was mainnet launch week in November 2025. The second was MON's 21–25% April surge to about $0.032 on $151–185M of daily volume, followed by EMO's April 15 high ([AO Trading](https://aotrading.io/blogs/monad-mon-surge-april-2026-on-chain-analysis)). The third was Monad's record DEX days of **$362M on August 31 and $513M on September 8**, around EMO's September 6 high ([Monad Aug 2026 highlights](https://monad.xyz/blog/monad-ecosystem-highlights-august-2026); [CoinMarketCap AI](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/monad/latest-updates/)).

The same timeline undercuts "sometimes leads that volume." Where dates exist, EMO peaked days after the chain-wide surge began. EMO's high came about a week after MON's April 8 print near $0.0326 ([MEXC News](https://www.mexc.com/tr-CT/news/1012837)). EMO bottomed on September 2, after the August 31 record, and its only "lead" is that the September 6 peak came two days before the larger September 8 record. That is a high-beta follower pattern. It is not conclusive, because the start of each EMO run cannot be dated without candles. Monad's DEX volume also appears dominated by DeFi and stablecoin flow, so the right benchmark would be nad.fun or meme-category volume, and no series for either exists.

The "new money" claim cannot be tested without wallet-level data. Holder count is the only proxy available. It rose from **5,759 on September 5 to 6,249 on September 29**, about 20 new addresses a day across the spike and the Emogotchi launch, then slowed to **6,299 by October 11**, about 4 a day ([Monadscan](https://monadscan.com/token/0x81a224f8a62f52bde942dbf23a56df77a10b7777); [Monadscan](https://monadscan.com/token/0x81a224f8a62f52bde942dbf23a56df77a10b7777?a=0xaD01C20d5886137e056775af56915de824c8fCe5)). That fits fresh wallets arriving on burst days. But a new address is not necessarily new money, and net counts hide who sold. If the claim is right, it cuts both ways: new buyers who arrived near the September high are now down about 56%, and holder growth slowed sharply afterwards. EMO has a discovery channel but a retention problem. You could settle this yourself by checking the first-seen dates of the wallets that bought on EMO's top-volume days.

## Conclusion

The real question is not whether EMO is strong among Monad memes. On survival, holders and product output, it plausibly is. The question is whether Monad gets a meme season at all, and the chain's record so far looks more like Berachain's or Sui's than Solana's or Base's. That makes EMO a high-beta option on a Monad spark that hasn't appeared yet, held through a supply event of about 1.4 times MON's float. Your building is real, but it is priced in pennies: the whole burn economy so far is worth less than one hour of EMO's peak-day volume. What building buys you, if the comparables hold, is better survival after a peak, not a higher peak.

Within your control, the evidence says two things matter more than the next feature. The first is credibility: accurate claims and published holder and burn data are the advantages a mascot meme can't copy. The second is converting reach into holders: 82K wallets reached against roughly 6,300 holders is the gap that decides whether a future burst leaves EMO with a larger base or just another retrace. If Monad does get a spark, a 10x is a reasonable bull case. A 100x would require EMO to become the chain's flagship and get a tier-1 listing, and a 1000x is not a planning scenario.
